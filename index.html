Exposure Statistics Tool

From raw sample results to a plain-language compliance call: data-quality check, sampling and analytical error, confidence limits, the 95th percentile with its UCL / UTL, exceedance fraction and Bayesian decision analysis against the OEL and the action level.

Lognormal methods as in AIHA's A Strategy for Assessing and Managing Occupational Exposures (4th ed.), IHSTAT / IHSTAT-Bayes and Expostats; sampling and analytical error as in the OSHA Technical Manual and NIOSH. Use it on its own or as part of a risk assessment.

03Data quality check the gatekeeper: do these samples belong together?

Statistics describe one exposure situation. Before any are run, confirm that every sample comes from the same similar exposure group under the same conditions. This is not a goodness-of-fit test: one sample a year over four years with different production rates fits a lognormal perfectly and still cannot be pooled.

07Sampling and analytical error each sample on its own

OSHA's single-sample decision rule: with the result as a fraction of the OEL (Y) and the sampling and analytical error (SAE), the lower and upper confidence limits are Y − SAE and Y + SAE. An overexposure is established when the LCL exceeds 1; compliance when the UCL is below 1; otherwise a possible overexposure. The SAE is 1.645 × the total CV (√(CVanalytical² + CVsampling²)) at 95% one-sided confidence.

10Decision settings

Overexposure risk is the probability that the 95th percentile exceeds the OEL, i.e. that more than 5% of exposures exceed it. 5% matches "95% confident the 95th percentile is below the OEL" (the UTL95,95 test); 30% is the threshold Expostats proposes for a poorly controlled exposure. Between the two the exposure is in the gray zone: more data, or more control.

12Method and references

What the tool does, in order
  1. Gatekeeper. Seven yes / no / unknown questions about the SEG, plus automatic checks: sample count, share of censored results, the time span and a Spearman trend test on dated results, Grubbs' outlier test on the log scale, and whether worker IDs allow a between-worker check. A "No" fails the gate and the statistics are withheld unless the assessor overrides (the report says so).
  2. Sampling and analytical error. Each sample's confidence limits against the OEL by the OSHA single-sample rule (SAE = 1.645 × total CV).
  3. Lognormal fit. Non-detects, above-range values and ranges by censored maximum likelihood (Nelder-Mead on the log-likelihood), or ROS, or substitution. GM, GSD; 95% CI on the GM (t) and the GSD (chi-square); AM with Cox's confidence limits; 95th percentile with the exact one-sided tolerance factor K (noncentral t) for the UTL95,95 and the lower limit; exceedance fraction with confidence limits through the noncentral t.
  4. Goodness of fit. Shapiro-Wilk W (Royston's AS R94) on the log and the arithmetic scale, with the probability-plot correlation and the Q-Q plot.
  5. Bayesian analysis. Gibbs sampler with data augmentation for censored values: flat prior on log GM, uniform prior on log GSD up to GSD 10 (as in Expostats). Gives 90% credible intervals, the overexposure risk P(X95 > OEL), P(AM > OEL), the probability of each AIHA category and, optionally, a decision prior over the categories (IHSTAT-Bayes). With one sample a prior on the GSD (default 2.5) stands in for the spread.
  6. Workers. One-way random-effects ANOVA on the log results (between- and within-worker GSD, R, the probability that a random worker's 95th percentile or mean exceeds the OEL) and the same model by Gibbs sampling.
  7. Comparison. Two groups: Welch's t-test on the logs (GM ratio and CI), an F-test on the GSDs, and the Bayesian probability that the second group's GM, 95th percentile and AM are lower.
  8. Non-parametric fallback. Median, empirical 95th percentile (Hazen), count above the OEL with an exact binomial CI, and the confidence that the maximum is an upper tolerance limit.
  9. Verdict. Go when the overexposure risk is below the go threshold; no-go when it is at or above the no-go threshold or the point estimate of the 95th percentile exceeds the OEL; otherwise the gray zone. The action level is judged the same way.
